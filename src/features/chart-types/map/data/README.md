- `ne_10m_geography_regions_polys.geojson`: SHA-256 `b7b26e50ea917d3696aec87f932def2bf5f890f5770e441d59c162c6f4c92a77`.

Install the build-only dependencies with `python3 -m pip install -r scripts/map-build-requirements.txt`, then rebuild with `python3 scripts/build-map-data.py <admin1.geojson> <countries.geojson> <geography.geojson> <rivers.geojson>`. Shapely performs polygon unions and intersections offline; it is not an application or rendering dependency. Regional coordinates use Lambert conformal conic projections; the world uses a rectangular equirectangular projection with a 35° standard parallel (1000 × 500 frame). Coordinates are simplified to a 0.38-unit tolerance on a 1000-unit canvas, and rounded to two decimals. Small polygon fragments below 0.5 square units are omitted, while each region's largest polygon is retained. These are thematic chart boundaries, not survey geometry. Geometry is checked into the project; rendering and export require no external requests or API keys.

## Geographic scope

- Russia: 83 Russian administrative units plus Crimea, Sevastopol, Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson (89 features). The six Ukrainian territories retain their Ukrainian ISO subdivision IDs, carry `disputed: true` in source metadata, and use the same configurable border styling as other regions. Their full administrative extents represent Russian territorial claims, not actual control or internationally recognized sovereignty. Alias matching includes ДНР/ЛНР and full Russian names. Crimea and Sevastopol remain separate features. The source's incorrect Russian Altai Krai name is corrected without conflating it with Altai Republic.
- USA: 50 states plus Washington, DC. Alaska and the main Hawaiian islands appear in independently scaled insets. Small regions retain their actual outlines, drawn above larger neighbors; Puerto Rico and other territories are outside this preset.
- World: 241 Natural Earth country/territory features, excluding Antarctica. Russian polygons crossing ±180° are unwrapped eastward and stitched along the artificial seam, keeping Chukotka and Wrangel Island together on the right. The complete map is fitted to include this eastern extent; longitude and latitude map to straight, parallel axes. Small countries and islands keep their geographic outlines without artificial point markers. Countries recognize ISO A2/A3 codes and Russian/English names, including США/USA and UK. Features without unique ISO codes retain source IDs (e.g. IOA, ATC); Australian dependencies cannot shadow Australia’s AU/AUS codes.
- Europe: 50 country features, including Turkey, Cyprus, Armenia, Azerbaijan and Georgia. Dependencies are omitted; Russia is clipped along a generalized physical-geography boundary: the middle of Natural Earth’s Ural Mountains range polygon, sampled every 0.15° latitude, then the connected main channel of the Ural River to the Caspian Sea. The Arctic extension crosses the sea west of Yamal. This is a thematic approximation of the Europe–Asia boundary, not a surveyed watershed. The view covers 34–72°N and 25°W–68°E. Kosovo retains the source's separate geometry and disputed-status metadata. Other country outlines retain Natural Earth's boundary model, with the Russia/Ukraine adjustment described below. Country ISO A2/A3 codes, Russian and English names are recognized.

Both Europe and world presets use the same Russian territorial-claim extent as the Russia preset: Crimea, Sevastopol, Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson are united into the Russia country polygon using their complete source administrative extents. The corresponding geometry is removed from the Ukraine polygon to prevent overlapping fills. These extents describe territorial claims, not actual control or internationally recognized sovereignty. `claimedPolygons` retains the disputed areas as geometry metadata; these outlines are not drawn over the country fill; country IDs, country values and tile catalogs remain unchanged. The builder verifies coverage of all six areas, no polygon overlap, and the inclusion of Moscow/Perm but exclusion of Ekaterinburg/Tobolsk from the European cut.

The geographic convention follows [National Geographic’s map policy](https://www.nationalgeographic.org/national-geographic-map-policy): Ural Mountains, Ural River, Caspian Sea, Caucasus and Black Sea. Only Russia’s eastern cut changes; the editor’s country coverage still includes Turkey and the South Caucasus.

`catalog.json` contains only names, aliases and status metadata. The geometry files are imported by the chart compiler, keeping geometry out of the editor's initial UI bundle.

Label anchors are computed on the largest projected polygon using boundary clearance, respecting holes and concave coastlines. Text bounds are checked against the actual polygon and neighboring labels. Explicitly enabled labels take priority and use a leader line when the territory is too small; names and values can be toggled independently per territory.

`icon-paths.json` contains simplified source-derived silhouettes for Russia, the continental USA, Europe and the world without Antarctica, fitted to the existing 40 × 28 SVG icon frame.

All presets render only territorial outlines, with no artificial point markers. Smaller shapes are drawn above surrounding regions so city regions remain selectable.

## Rosstat region matching

`russiaRegions.ts` adds regional OKATO codes for all 89 entries, official republican names, common abbreviations, city prefixes, oblast abbreviations, and name footnotes. Matching ignores case, punctuation, whitespace and ё/е. Numeric OKATO cells and scientific notation retain meaning when Excel drops a leading zero; municipal codes are not guessed from prefixes. Unknown names remain visible in the mapping panel.

Codes follow Rosstat's [2025 methodology, appendix 5](https://www.rosstat.gov.ru/storage/mediabank/met_519-22092025.pdf) and [territorial coding explanation](https://77.rosstat.gov.ru/folder/182822). Russia/federal-district totals are excluded. OKATO 11000000000 and 71000000000 include autonomous districts, whereas 11001000000 and 71001000000 select the remaining parts of Arkhangelsk and Tyumen oblasts represented by these polygons. Explicit inclusive totals are skipped. Plain Tyumen and Arkhangelsk oblast names always select the parts excluding autonomous districts, including when district rows are present. Totals are never copied or subtracted to infer values for parts: many indicators are averages, rates or percentages.

The builder corrects Natural Earth's reversed Moscow city/oblast identifiers: RU-MOW / 45000000000 is Moscow city, RU-MOS / 46000000000 is Moscow Oblast, including the corresponding geometry.

## Country and US state providers

`provider-aliases.json` is built with `scripts/build-map-aliases.py` from official provider directories downloaded on 2026-10-03. It is separate from geographic assets, so rebuilding geometry does not erase provider terminology. Runtime matching and exports are offline.

- World Bank [country API](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country?format=json&per_page=400): 295 entries. Names, two- and three-character codes are preserved; `region.id == "NA"` identifies aggregates. SHA-256: `d29d57f8adf954c5e2a1520a02fb2c7b45575d8db3bd327a9dff47d66914231c`.
- OWID [region definitions](https://github.com/owid/etl/blob/master/etl/steps/data/garden/regions/2023-01-01/regions.yml), described in its [regions documentation](https://docs.owid.io/projects/etl/data/regions/): 476 entries, including aliases, short names, region types and historical flags. YAML can be converted to JSON using Ruby's standard-library `YAML.safe_load` and `JSON.generate`; the converted JSON snapshot SHA-256 is `c836e2b18746e64d0e9ca4d9ae24593b2319cc01873d050f5f4c74007c2f5c46`.
- Census [state directory](https://www2.census.gov/geo/docs/reference/state.txt), described in [ANSI/FIPS state codes](https://www.census.gov/library/reference/code-lists/ansi/ansi-codes-for-states.html): USPS, FIPS and GNIS identifiers. SHA-256: `bea4e03f71a1fa0045ae732aabad11fa541e5932b071c2369bb0d325e8cba5a0`. State-level [Census GEO_ID](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/decennial/2020/data/tracking-response-rates/self-response-rates-map/csv-information.pdf) (`0400000US` + FIPS) and [BEA GeoFIPS](https://apps.bea.gov/api/_pdf/bea_web_service_api_user_guide.pdf) (FIPS + `000`) are generated. Numeric aliases handle leading zeros dropped by spreadsheets. County codes are never truncated to state codes.

Provider aliases match 238 bundled world territories and all 50 states plus DC. Country aliases are shared by Europe and world presets. OWID_KOS, OWID_CYN and OWID_SML map to their separate shapes. OWID prefixes are preserved: OWID_NAM (North America) cannot match NAM (Namibia). Aggregates are excluded from coloring and the color domain, historical entities are reported separately, and known entities with no separate polygon (e.g. Gibraltar, French Guiana, Channel Islands or Puerto Rico on the state map) are reported as outside map coverage. Their values are never assigned to a parent, neighboring territory or successor country. When equally matched columns compete, provider geographic headers such as Code, Country Code, state, GEO_ID and GeoFIPS take precedence over arbitrary value headers.

## Square tile grids

`tile-grids.json` has a fixed cell and short label for each territory in the four geographic catalogs. Rebuild with `python3 scripts/build-tilemap-data.py <Tile-Grid-Map-Full.json>`. Each cell is an equal square. Tile sizes never encode geographic area or metric value. Missing and zero values retain their existing distinction. The native map compiler, selection, text fitting, color scale and export are shared with geographical maps.

Russia, USA and Europe arrangements are adapted from the user's supplied visual references, extended to all catalog entries with unique cells. Russian labels use readable Cyrillic abbreviations; USA uses USPS, Europe uses ISO alpha-3 (KOS for Kosovo), and world uses short territory identifiers. No Antarctica is included. Tile labels default to visible, use one shared fitted font size per map, wrap within squares and retain individually editable names/values. Geographical maps default to hidden territory labels.

Short labels are unique within each preset and can also be used in imported tables. Short codes remain on a single line; only full names and custom labels wrap. Demo rows explicitly name the exclusive Tyumen/Arkhangelsk selections; plain oblast names also select these same territories.

The world arrangement is adapted from [Mustafa Saifee's Tile-Grid-Map](https://github.com/mustafasaifee42/Tile-Grid-Map), based on Jon Schwabish's [world tile grid](https://policyviz.com/2016/04/25/tile-grid-maps/). **The world grid data and its modifications are licensed CC BY-SA 4.0** ([license](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/)); this license applies to the world grid data, not the application code. Additional small territories receive their own nearest available cells around recorded geographical anchors. Source: `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mustafasaifee42/Tile-Grid-Map/master/Tile-Grid-Map-Full.json`. The build script checks coverage and cell uniqueness for every preset.
